# Gonggong–Buzhou / Nüwa–Mends-Heaven Fusion Chronology v1.2

Status: **RESEARCH NOTE / NOT HISTORICAL FACT CANON**

## Research question
When did the originally separable motifs `共工触不周山` and `女娲补天` become welded into one causal narrative, and through which textual / learned-tradition layer?

## Current evidence ladder

### S — Excavated Warring-States layer: Changsha Zidanku Chu Silk Manuscript
Common modern readings of the creation section place Fuxi/Nüwa, their four sons, Zhurong, and Gonggong inside one cosmological/time-order system. In the surviving text Gonggong participates in calendrical/time ordering; the preserved passage does **not** present the later canonical chain `Gonggong breaks Buzhou -> Nüwa repairs the damage`.

Interpretation gate: readings of the Chu silk manuscript are philologically disputed. Use this layer to show an earlier alternative cosmological role for Gonggong, not to assert a single uncontested translation.

### A — Western Han: Huainanzi preserves the motifs separately
`淮南子·天文训`:
- Gonggong contends with Zhuanxu.
- He strikes Buzhou.
- Heaven's pillar breaks / earth's cord snaps.
- The passage explains the northwest tilt of heaven and southeast drainage of the earth.
- It does **not** bring Nüwa into that passage.

`淮南子·览冥训`:
- The four poles fail and the nine regions split.
- Fire and flood ravage the world.
- Nüwa melts five-colored stones to repair heaven, cuts the Ao's feet to establish the four poles, kills the black dragon, and uses reed ash against the flood.
- The passage does **not** name Gonggong as the cause.

`淮南子·原道训` preserves yet another Gonggong configuration: Gonggong strikes Buzhou but contends with **Gaoxin (高辛)** rather than Zhuanxu, and the defeated line is described as `宗族残灭，继嗣绝祀`.

Thus the same Western-Han work preserves multiple Gonggong configurations plus the Nüwa repair complex; the currently verified passages do not form one stable single biography or one single Gonggong-causes-Nüwa-repair sequence.

### A+ — Eastern-Han Huainanzi commentary: Gao You supports title/lineage semantics while Nüwa remains separately framed
Gao You's commentary tradition is unusually important for the chronology audit.

On `天文训` at the Gonggong/Zhuanxu passage, the line is glossed in substance:
- `共工，官名`
- Gonggong held status in the Fuxi/Shennong-era horizon.
- `其后子孙` later became powerful and contended with Zhuanxu.

On `原道训`, Gao You distinguishes that Gonggong from the Yao-era Gonggong (`非尧时共工也`).

This is direct evidence that at least one Eastern-Han exegetical tradition did **not** require every Gonggong notice to refer to one immortal individual. `共工` could be treated as an office/name-bearing lineage whose descendants continue the political identity, while different textual Gonggong traditions are explicitly distinguished.

On `览冥训`, Gao You's commentary instead glosses Nüwa as an ancient female sovereign associated with Fuxi and explains the repair under a `三皇时` teaching (`师说如此`). It treats the Ao/four-pole episode inside the Nüwa repair tradition and does not insert Gonggong as the cause of that disaster passage.

### A — Eastern Han: Lunheng gives the earliest extant explicit fusion currently verified
`论衡·谈天` opens by attributing a narrative to `儒书`:
- Gonggong loses the contest with Zhuanxu.
- He strikes Buzhou and breaks the heavenly pillar / earthly cord.
- Nüwa then melts five-colored stones to repair heaven and cuts the Ao's feet to establish the four poles.

Wang Chong later makes the linkage even more explicit in his own reasoning: `共工得折之，女娲得补之也`.

This is the **earliest extant explicit fusion currently verified in this audit**.

Critical caveat: Wang Chong does not claim authorship. His wording `儒书言` and `此久远之文，世间是之言也` implies that he regarded the fused account as an already circulating older textual/traditional claim.

### `儒书` correction: category label, not a demonstrated book title
A corpus-level reading of Wang Chong's usage changes the question. Elsewhere in `论衡`, Wang repeatedly uses formulations such as `儒书言` / `儒书称` for mutually unrelated learned stories: Yellow Emperor/dragon traditions, Shun/Yu burial traditions, Xiongqu shooting a stone, and other transmitted claims. Modern critical commentary also treats Wang's `儒书` broadly enough to include heterogeneous learned, technical, cosmological, legendary, and miscellaneous written traditions.

Therefore the target is **not** “find the lost book titled *Rushu*”. The correct research question is:

> What textual, exegetical, or circulating learned-tradition ancestor(s) stand behind Wang Chong's generic `儒书言` formulation in `谈天`?

Current state:
- VERIFIED: the fusion existed by Wang Chong's time.
- NOT VERIFIED: Wang Chong invented the fusion.
- NOT ESTABLISHED: `儒书` denotes one specific lost book.
- OPEN: whether Wang paraphrases a lost source, combines several learned traditions, reproduces an exegetical synthesis, or reports a widely circulating learned narrative.

### A — Modern critical commentary does not resolve the ancestor into one named book
Huang Hui's `论衡校释` collates parallels from `淮南子·原道训 / 天文训 / 览冥训`, the received `列子·汤问`, and the ancient Huainanzi commentary traditions. In the material currently checked, it does **not** identify one named lost book that can simply be equated with Wang Chong's `儒书`.

Therefore the safest textual-critical position remains:
- Wang Chong's passage is a **terminus ante quem** for the fused narrative.
- `儒书` is better treated as a category-level citation formula unless a specific source is independently identified.
- It is not justified to equate `儒书` with one extant Huainanzi chapter, because the extant Huainanzi distributes the damage and repair complexes across different passages and preserves conflicting Gonggong chronologies.

Critical-commentary lead:

### B — Extant Liezi, Tang Wen: juxtaposition without causal fusion
The received `列子·汤问` puts Nüwa's repair first and says **`其后`** Gonggong struck Buzhou. This preserves the two motifs in one cosmological discussion while explicitly ordering Gonggong *after* Nüwa, which is incompatible with the later causal chain `Gonggong damage -> Nüwa repair`.

Textual-date caveat: the received Liezi has a difficult transmission/redaction history; do not use its current wording as a securely pre-Qin chronological anchor without a separate textual-history audit.

Primary-text lead:
- https://ctext.org/liezi/tang-wen

### B — Tang: Sima Zhen's Supplement to the Shiji canonizes a smooth causal narrative
`补史记三皇本纪` narrates:
- Gonggong fights Zhurong and loses.
- He strikes Buzhou; the heavenly pillar breaks and earth's support fails.
- `女娲乃` melts five-colored stones to repair heaven, establishes the four poles, and stops the flood.

The received text even annotates the episode as deriving from the Huainanzi. But the Western-Han Huainanzi passages currently verified preserve Gonggong/Buzhou and Nüwa/repair in different chapters rather than this single smooth causal sequence. Sima Zhen therefore represents a later synthesis/canonization layer, not proof that the Huainanzi itself already narrated the combined plot in this exact form.

## Working model v1.2
1. **Warring States:** excavated Chu cosmology can place Nüwa and Gonggong inside the same broad cosmic-order system without making Gonggong the destroyer whose damage Nüwa repairs.
2. **Western Han:** Huainanzi preserves Gonggong/Buzhou variants and a Nüwa/cosmic-repair motif in separate textual locations; even Gonggong's opponent is not stable across chapters.
3. **Eastern-Han commentary:** Gao You can explicitly understand `共工` as an office/name-bearing lineage with later descendants and distinguish separate chronological Gonggong traditions, while Nüwa's four-pole repair remains separately framed.
4. **By Eastern Han:** Lunheng attests an already circulating learned narrative in which the damage and repair motifs are explicitly chained together.
5. **Later transmission:** received Liezi preserves a non-causal/reversed ordering, proving the merged causal version was not the only surviving arrangement.
6. **Tang historical synthesis:** Sima Zhen turns the chain into a clean dynastic/mythic narrative and helps canonize the now-familiar sequence.

## Strongest current inference
The familiar modern plot should not be projected wholesale backward into the Western-Han Huainanzi. A more economical model is **motif convergence / textual fusion across circulating learned traditions**, attested explicitly by Wang Chong and later narratively smoothed.

The Gao You evidence strengthens a second proposition: **multiple Gonggong generations / traditions are textually defensible.** A lineage/title model is cleaner than forcing all Gonggong episodes into one biological lifespan.
