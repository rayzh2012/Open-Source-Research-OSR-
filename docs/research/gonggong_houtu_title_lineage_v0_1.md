# Gonggong / Houtu Title-Lineage Audit v0.2

Status: **TEXTUAL RESEARCH NOTE — NOT HISTORICAL-REALITY CLAIM**

## Question
Do the early transmitted sources require `共工` and `后土` to be fixed personal names belonging to one biological individual/generation, or do they preserve office/title/lineage semantics compatible with multiple generations?

## A. `共工` can be read as an office/name-bearing lineage in Han commentary
In the Gao You commentary tradition on `淮南子·天文训`, the Gonggong/Zhuanxu passage is glossed:

- `共工，官名`
- Gonggong is placed in an earlier Fuxi/Shennong-era horizon.
- `其后子孙` later contend with Zhuanxu.

This makes a one-body-for-all-Gonggong-episodes model unnecessary at the level of ancient exegesis itself.

### Attribution / transmission gate
The transmitted `淮南鸿烈解` material preserves Xu Shen / Gao You ancient-commentary layers with a complicated transmission history. Page-level headings or edition labels are not sufficient for sentence-level attribution. Later collation and quotations identify the `共工，官名 ... 其后子孙` line, as well as the `原道训` distinction `非尧时共工也`, as **Gao You commentary**. Future provenance should therefore cite the exact sentence and collation chain rather than infer authorship from a page title.

Primary/commentary lead:
- https://ctext.org/wiki.pl?chapter=594804&if=gb&remap=gb

## B. `后土` is explicitly an office in Zuo Zhuan
`左传·昭公二十九年` first defines the Five Offices:

- 木正曰句芒
- 火正曰祝融
- 金正曰蓐收
- 水正曰玄冥
- **土正曰后土**

It then identifies office-holders:

- `颛顼氏有子曰犁，为祝融`
- `共工氏有子曰句龙，为后土`

The syntax strongly supports:
- `句龙` = the named descendant / person;
- `后土` = the office/title (`土正`).

Primary lead:
- https://ctext.org/chun-qiu-zuo-zhuan/zhao-gong-er-shi-jiu-nian/zhs

## C. Li Ji compresses the personal name into the office title
`礼记·祭法` gives:

`共工氏之霸九州也，其子曰后土，能平九州，故祀以为社。`

Compared with the more explicit Zuo Zhuan formulation `其子曰句龙，为后土`, this looks like a compressed tradition in which the office-name can replace the individual's personal name.

Primary lead:
- https://ctext.org/text.pl?if=gb&node=11683&show=parallel

## D. Later ritual commentary explicitly recognizes person/office distinction
Later ritual exegesis states in substance that Goulong held the Houtu office and that the terms can denote different ontological layers: person, official title, earth deity, and She association. This is later evidence, but it demonstrates that premodern commentators themselves recognized the ambiguity rather than assuming `后土` was always a fixed personal name.

## E. Huainanzi itself preserves incompatible Gonggong chronologies
`淮南子·原道训`:
- Gonggong strikes Buzhou.
- contends with **Gaoxin (高辛)** for imperial position.
- the line says his clan is destroyed and succession cut off.

Gao You's commentary on this passage says this Gonggong is from the Fuxi/Shennong horizon and is **not the Yao-era Gonggong** (`非尧时共工也`).

`淮南子·天文训` instead says Gonggong contends with **Zhuanxu**.

This internal variation is hard to reconcile with a single literal biography but natural under a model of multiple traditions, title-bearing groups, or generational compression.

## F. `句龙佐颛顼` is a later quoted/expanded variant, not the extant Zuo Zhuan wording
Ying Shao's Eastern-Han `风俗通义·祀典·社神` quotes a version in substance:

`共工有子曰勾龙，佐颛顼，能平九土，为后土 ...`

The extant `左传·昭公二十九年` does **not** contain the words `佐颛顼`; it only has `共工氏有子曰句龙，为后土`. Therefore `句龙佐颛顼` is useful as evidence for an Eastern-Han quotation/expanded tradition, but it must not be silently back-projected into the received Zuo Zhuan text.

Narrative value for Hongming: this is a strong option for a post-conflict political settlement in which a Gonggong descendant is incorporated into Zhuanxu's administrative order as Earth Corrector. Historical-text status remains **VARIANT / LATER QUOTATION**, not primary-text FACT.

## Textual tension that must remain visible
The `原道训` wording `宗族残灭，继嗣绝祀` conflicts with any naive continuous biological lineage extending from that exact defeated Gonggong branch. Do not erase this contradiction.

Competing explanations to keep open:
1. different Gonggong traditions were conflated under one title;
2. `宗族残灭` refers to one branch/political house, not every bearer of the title;
3. the Houtu genealogy belongs to a different chronological Gonggong tradition;
4. later genealogical harmonization merged originally separate figures.

No option is promoted without additional evidence.

## Working model for Hongming compatibility
A text-compatible fictional implementation can distinguish:

`GONGGONG OFFICE / CLAN IDENTITY`
→ multiple holders / branches across generations
→ one descendant/person `句龙`
→ holds office `后土 (土正)`
→ later cultic language can collapse person + office + deity into the single label `后土`.

This model avoids forcing every Gonggong notice and every Houtu notice into one biological lifespan while preserving the transmitted textual ambiguity.

## Truth-status summary
- Gao You commentary says `共工，官名` and mentions later descendants — **TEXTUAL FACT (commentarial)**.
- Gao You commentary distinguishes one Gonggong from the Yao-era Gonggong — **TEXTUAL FACT (commentarial)**.
- Zuo Zhuan defines `土正曰后土` and `句龙，为后土` — **TEXTUAL FACT**.
- Li Ji says Gonggong's son is `后土` — **TEXTUAL FACT**, compatible with title-for-person compression.
- Ying Shao preserves a `句龙佐颛顼` expanded quotation — **TEXTUAL FACT OF EASTERN-HAN QUOTATION / VARIANT**, not extant Zuo Zhuan wording.
- One historical office system actually existed exactly as described — **NOT ESTABLISHED**.
- All Gonggong notices refer to one person — **NOT REQUIRED BY THE SOURCES**.
- Multiple-generation Gonggong is textually defensible — **STRONG INTERPRETIVE MODEL**.
